Why trust the numbers

We graded ourselves before charging you a dollar.

Before launch, we re-played two full seasons of a real league and graded every one of our own start/sit calls against the actual box scores — 2,056 of them. Across all of them we hit 53.5%, and five of six confidence buckets came in wrong. The reason is the most useful thing we learned: we couldn't see who was inactive. That single failure is why the live report refuses to print a confidence number until it has verified both players are active — and why win probability is still withheld entirely.

What the season actually looks like

Tuesday morning, ninety seconds. You open one email. At the top is a three-box checklist — the lineup changes worth making, what to bid on the waiver everyone's chasing, and the one player whose news you need to check later in the week. Tick the boxes and you're done; you've made the calls that matter before your coffee's cold.

Everything under the checklist is the evidence for it, there for the weeks you want to argue with us: your rival's lineup beside yours with their weak spots flagged, what a waiver claim actually costs in your league and who can afford to outbid you, and the one coin-flip call decided with the reasoning attached.

Then Monday, we grade our own homework. Every call we gave you gets graded against the real box score and posted publicly — the misses too. That's the whole routine. No app to check, no dashboard to babysit.
