How your report works

Every Tuesday: your lineup decided, the week's closest call, your game plan and an if/then for late news. Here's what each part means and how to check any call.

Check my team

01What you get, and when

  • Tuesday morning: one report for your roster, under the scoring you pick (full PPR, half PPR or standard): every slot decided, every close call explained, the odds where we can give them (which rows get them), and a plan for late news. Your lineup comes first, and setting it takes about a minute. The rest is the why.
  • Saturday, only if needed: if Friday's injury report changes your lineup, the Saturday check tells you the swap. No news, no email.
  • That's the whole inbox. The day you join, your welcome and your first report. Then one email every Tuesday, a second only when Saturday's check changes your lineup, and a confirmation button when you change your roster. No promotions.
  • Your roster, your way: you paste it when you sign up, and again whenever it changes. No login, no league connection.

The percentage on a row
The chance your starter outscores your best bench player at that spot. 50% is a coin flip, and the bar starts there. A 70% call is a clear favorite, not a certainty: it can still lose.
Rows with no percentage
A row prints no odds for reasons like these:
  • Not playing, or on the injury report. We give odds only when both players' teams play this week and neither was listed questionable, doubtful or out on last week's injury report.
  • Reserve list or practice squad. Neither player may sit on a reserve list or the practice squad.
  • Too few games. Both need enough games on record: three this season.
  • A defense. Never on defenses: they get a projected score only.
  • No bench player there. With nobody to compare against, there is no chance to state.
  • A rising bench player. We hold them where your bench player there is getting more of the ball lately and his projection still counts his earlier games as much.
Those rows still get a projection, and the report says why there's no number.
Early-season rules

There are no odds in Week 1, and each row shows last season's scoring instead of a projection. In Weeks 2 and 3, for full-PPR, 12-team standard lineups that start a kicker and a defense, last season counts toward a player's games on record.

03Where the numbers come from

Your roster and your scoring settings, plus public NFL data from nflverse, an open project: box scores, the schedule and the league's injury reports. A player's projection starts from his recent games, blends in last season, and allows for byes, announced injuries and how many points his game should produce. In Week 1 your slots are ordered by last season's scoring. Then the report sets your starter against your best bench player at that spot.

We have no inside information and no view into your league's other teams. Your report is built only from your roster and public data.

04How to check us

  • Every call shows the projection behind it and the player's recent games, so you can look up the box scores and see for yourself.
  • Every report after your first shows how the previous report's calls with odds finished.
  • The sample report is a real one, built for a sample roster from a week of the 2024 season. Read it beside your own league app.The this week's report is a real one, for a sample roster, rebuilt every Tuesday of the season. Read it beside your own league app.

05What it doesn't do

It decides who starts. It doesn't rank draft picks, value trades or tell you who to pick up; Rising roles only points at who's getting more work. Football is noisy: a good call can lose, and a bad one can win. What we add: every close call decided with a reason, and late news planned for, before kickoff.

06Who's behind it

Beat Your League is independent and small: one fantasy player built it, after too many lineups set at 12:55 on Sunday. It's paid for only by the people who buy it: no ads and no selling your data. Questions or something that looks wrong: hello@beatyourleague.com.
