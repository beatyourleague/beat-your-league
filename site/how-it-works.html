What lands in your inbox, what each part means, where the numbers come from, and how to check any call yourself. You paste your roster when you sign up, and again whenever it changes (every report tells you how). No login, no league connection.

The percentage on a row
The chance your starter outscores your best bench player at that spot. 50% is a coin flip, and the bar starts there. A 70% call is a strong lean, not a lock: it can still lose.

03Where the numbers come from

Your roster and your scoring settings, plus public NFL data from nflverse, an open project: box scores, the schedule and the league's injury reports. In Weeks 4 to 16, when the week's game lines are in, a player's projection starts from his recent games, blends in last season, allows for byes and injuries the league has announced, and for how many points his game is expected to produce. Otherwise it leans mostly on this season's games, and in Week 1 your slots are ordered by last season's scoring. Then the report sets your starter against your best bench player at that spot.

That's all of it. We have no inside information and no view into your league's other teams. Your report is built only from your roster and public data.

04How to check us

  • Every call shows the projection behind it and the player's recent games, so you can look up the box scores and see for yourself.
  • Every report after your first shows how the previous report's calls with odds finished.

05What it doesn't do

It decides who starts. It doesn't rank draft picks, value trades or tell you who to pick up; Rising roles only points at who's getting more work. And football is noisy: a good call can lose, and a bad one can win. What we add: every close call decided with a reason, and late news planned for, before kickoff.
