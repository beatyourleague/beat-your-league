How your report works

Every Tuesday: every slot decided, the week's closest call explained, what to do and by when, and a plan for late news. Here's what each part means, where the numbers come from and how to check any call. You paste your roster when you sign up, and again whenever it changes. No login, no league connection.

The percentage on a row
The chance your starter outscores your best bench player at that spot. 50% is a coin flip, and the bar starts there. A 70% call is a clear favorite, not a certainty: it can still lose.
Rows with no percentage
A row prints no odds for reasons like these:
  • Not playing, or on the injury report. We give odds only when both players' teams play this week and neither was listed questionable, doubtful or out on last week's injury report.
  • Reserve list or practice squad. Neither player may sit on a reserve list or the practice squad.
  • Too few games. Both need enough games on record: three this season, or — in Weeks 2 and 3, for full-PPR, 12-team standard lineups that start a kicker and a defense — last season counted in, and never in Week 1.
  • A defense. Never on defenses: they get a projection only.
  • No bench player there. With nobody to compare against, there is no chance to state.
  • A rising bench player. We hold them where your bench player there is getting more of the ball lately and his projection still counts his earlier games as much.

03Where the numbers come from

Your roster and your scoring settings, plus public NFL data from nflverse, an open project: box scores, the schedule and the league's injury reports. In Weeks 4 to 16, once each game's expected scoring is known, a player's projection starts from his recent games, blends in last season, allows for byes and injuries the league has announced, and for how many points his game is expected to produce. Otherwise it leans mostly on this season's games, and in Week 1 your slots are ordered by last season's scoring. Then the report sets your starter against your best bench player at that spot.

That's all of it. We have no inside information and no view into your league's other teams. Your report is built only from your roster and public data.

05What it doesn't do

It decides who starts. It doesn't rank draft picks, value trades or tell you who to pick up; Rising roles only points at who's getting more work. And football is noisy: a good call can lose, and a bad one can win. What we add: every close call decided with a reason, and late news planned for, before kickoff.
